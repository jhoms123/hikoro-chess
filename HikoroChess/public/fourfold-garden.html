How to play

  • Each player starts with 10 pieces: six Flowers and four Tools. On a normal turn, place one reserve piece. If at least one legal active ability is available after placement, you must use one before the turn can end; you cannot skip it to pick up a piece or finish. If no legal active ability is available, you may pick up one of your own older pieces or finish your turn. A picked-up piece returns to your reserve. If your placement uses your last reserve piece and does not win, you must also pick up one of your own pieces before ending that same turn. If your new piece is captured immediately and you have no other piece on the board, that capture already removes it and your turn ends. If you start a turn with no reserve pieces, your whole turn is a mandatory pickup. You normally cannot pick up the piece you just placed; if it is your only piece on the board, you may pick it up to keep the game moving.
  • Same-owner Flowers resonate with Flowers on the Flower wheel, and same-owner Tools resonate with Tools on the Tool wheel. Flower–Tool pairs use the chart below: every pair is marked Harmony or Dissonance. A live mixed Harmony needs a clear row or column; it may be diagonal only when its Flower is a Rose or Chrysanthemum. Dissonance needs a clear row or column, may link either owner, and counts against both endpoints. Dissonance can suppress Harmony links and affect repetition scoring, but it does not block active abilities. Peony and Knotweed do not convert mixed Dissonance. A Peony converts two same-owner Flower Dissonance links on opposite sides into Counterpoint Harmony between the outer Flowers; Knotweed does the same for Tools. A clear Flower Harmony ray from either member of an active, adjacent Vine–Bamboo pair may pass through blockers; this Flower-only shortcut does not open mixed links. Rose and Vine cannot share a row or column, and Rose and Chrysanthemum cannot share a diagonal, regardless of ownership or blockers.
  • A closed, non-crossing Harmony ring of three or more distinct same-owner pieces captures enemy pieces strictly inside it; captured pieces return to their owners' reserves. Any ring can mix Flowers and Tools and can use orthogonal or permitted diagonal Harmony links. Enclose the gold center point with any such ring to win. If the same complete position occurs three times, the game ends and the player with the higher Harmony-to-Dissonance ratio wins; equal ratios draw. The position includes the player to move, reserves, used abilities, and active special links.
  • Garden thresholds are fixed and visible: one Dissonance ray suppresses a Red Flower's Harmony links, two suppress a Neutral Flower's, and three suppress a White Flower's. Any live Dissonance, including a mixed Flower–Tool ray, suppresses a Tool's Harmony links. Mixed Dissonance counts at both endpoints. The colored petals extend one ring farther, shrinking the Neutral corners.
  • Orchid pushes one adjacent enemy Flower directly away into an empty point. Wheel rotates its full eight-point ring clockwise when all eight points are on the board and none is a Rock. Fan pushes a straight contiguous row or column of up to 3 tiles one point away into an empty point (4 from Neutral); Rocks cannot move. Bell reaches 2 points (3 from Neutral): it pulls an enemy tile along a clear row or column into the empty point nearest the Bell. Dissonance can suppress Harmony links, but it does not block any legal active ability. A placement or tool movement that would create a prohibited alignment is illegal. Tools can be part of a capturing or center-winning ring.
  • Orchid, Wheel, Fan, and Bell each have one active ability use; whenever a legal active ability is available after a placement, one must be used before the turn can end. If a used ability piece is picked up or captured, it remains spent when returned to reserve. Rose, Chrysanthemum, Vine, Bamboo, Peony, and Knotweed have passive effects that resolve automatically; Peony and Knotweed do not spend a turn converting links. This compact edition uses ten pieces: Rose, Vine, Chrysanthemum, Bamboo, Orchid, Peony, Knotweed, Wheel, Fan, and Bell. Picking up one of your own blockers can expose or break rays. There are no round counters or temporary range bonuses.
Tip: When your last reserve placement does not win, use any legal active ability first, then make the required pickup. The pickup returns a piece to your reserve and ends your turn. Use “Undo” to take back your whole last turn.
Compact rules: the circular board has 109 playable points. Any simple ring of at least three same-owner pieces enclosing the center wins; live Flower, Tool, and mixed Harmony links can form its boundary. Otherwise, the third occurrence of an identical full position ends the game by each player's active Harmony ÷ Dissonance ratio. With no Dissonance and at least one Harmony, the ratio is infinite; with neither link it is zero. Pickups and non-winning captures return pieces to reserves. If both players complete a center ring in the same resolution, the player whose turn it is wins the center.

Balanced links: Every Flower has 6 resonant and 6 dissonant Flower relationships. The ordinary Tool wheel gives Harmony at one or two steps; the Knotweed–Wheel and Knotweed–Fan exceptions make all four active Tools mutually resonant. Every active Flower has 2 Harmony and 2 Dissonance links in the mixed chart, and every active Tool has 3 of each. Same-type pairings count as Dissonance.

Special connections: Rose and Chrysanthemum can make a clear diagonal Harmony link with a resonant friendly Flower or a Tool marked Harmony in the mixed chart. Adjacent friendly Vine and Bamboo let their Flower–Flower rays pass through blockers; mixed links still need a clear line. If a Peony is between two of its owner's Flowers that each dissonate with it on opposite sides of a clear row or column, those two Dissonance links are replaced by a Counterpoint Harmony between the outer Flowers. Knotweed does the same for two of its owner's Tools. Counterpoint removes those two same-group Dissonance links, ignores the outer pair's usual type relationship, and exists only while the pivot and both outer pieces remain active. It does not convert mixed Dissonance.

Flower, Tool, and mixed Harmony links can all form rings. An ordinary Harmony link needs a clear row or column, except when a Flower endpoint is Rose or Chrysanthemum; Dissonance always stays orthogonal and may link either owner. Mixed Dissonance contributes to the link-suppression threshold of both endpoints and to each represented owner's repetition score. Counterpoint is drawn as a dotted violet line and recalculated automatically; no marker or once-per-game conversion token is needed. Harmony never changes an ability's range. The alignment restrictions apply even through blockers: Rose–Vine cannot share a row or column, and Rose–Chrysanthemum cannot share a diagonal.

Repetition tiebreak: Count each active same-owner Flower, Tool, or mixed Harmony link once for that player. Count each active Dissonance link against every owner represented at either endpoint (so a cross-owner Dissonance counts against both). Compare Harmony ÷ Dissonance. A positive Harmony count with zero Dissonance is ∞; zero Harmony and zero Dissonance is 0. Equal ratios after the third occurrence are a draw.
